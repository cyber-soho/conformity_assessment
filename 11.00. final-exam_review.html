1.1 Definition and objectives of conformity assessment

Conformity assessment is the demonstration that specified requirements are fulfilled. This definition comes from ISO/IEC 17000:2020 (International Organization for Standardization / International Electrotechnical Commission), the vocabulary standard of conformity assessment: https://www.iso.org/standard/73029.html. In practice it is a structured comparison, based on evidence, between what is required and what actually exists.

Every assessment follows the same chain of five links, always in the same order:

Link Meaning Example: laptops must lock after 15 minutes
Requirement A need or expectation that is stated, implied or obligatory (law, contract, standard, internal policy) Acceptable Use Policy, section 4.2; CIS (Center for Internet Security) Windows 11 Benchmark: "Interactive logon: Machine inactivity limit" set to 900 seconds or fewer
Control The measure that should make the requirement true (setting, procedure, practice) The registry value InactivityTimeoutSecs pushed by policy
Test The planned procedure applied to the control (document review, interview, observation, technical check) Read the value and compare it with 900
Evidence Verifiable records produced by the test; must be sufficient, reliable and relevant A dated text file: requirement, test, observed value, computer, time
Finding The result of evaluating the evidence against the requirement One row of the findings table

A finding has one of three forms:

  • Conformity — the requirement is met. A conformity is a finding too: it proves what works.
  • Non-conformity — the requirement is not met. It is major when the control is absent or has broken down (no screen-lock policy at all in Intune), and minor when it is an isolated lapse in a control that otherwise works (one laptop out of ten sampled without the setting).
  • Observation — no requirement is broken, but the auditor notes a weakness or an opportunity for improvement.

Three more terms complete the vocabulary. A corrective action removes the cause of a non-conformity and prevents it from coming back; it is verified afterwards. The audit trail is the chronological record (evidence files, timestamps, names) that allows another person to reconstruct how each finding was reached. Conformity means the fulfilment of any requirement, while compliance is used for mandatory external obligations (laws, regulations, contracts).

One rule applies from the first lab to the last: the auditor observes, the administrator fixes. An auditor who changes a setting during a test destroys the evidence of the state that was found.

1.2 Why conformity assessment matters in cybersecurity management

An assessment with dated evidence is the proof of due diligence: the reasonable, documented steps a prudent organization takes to protect the information it holds. Without it, the organization faces legal exposure — penalties, orders and liabilities when a mandatory obligation is not met.

  • Breach notification is a legal duty after certain incidents: PIPEDA (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act) asks for a report "as soon as feasible", Québec's Law 25 asks for prompt notification, and the GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) asks for notification within 72 hours where feasible.
  • Each law has a regulator: the OPC (Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/) for PIPEDA, the CAI (Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec, https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/) for Law 25, the CRTC (Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission, https://www.crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/CASL/spam.htm) for CASL (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation), and the EU (European Union) supervisory authorities for the GDPR.
  • A contractual obligation binds through a contract instead of a law: PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) binds a merchant through its agreement with the acquiring bank. Small merchants validate it with an SAQ (Self-Assessment Questionnaire).
  • The security posture is the overall state of the controls measured at a given moment, and the improvement cycle (findings → corrective actions → verification → next assessment) is what turns activity into measurable progress.

1.3 Types of audits and assessments

ISO 19011 (International Organization for Standardization guidelines for auditing management systems, https://www.iso.org/standard/88984.html) defines an audit as a systematic, independent and documented process for obtaining objective evidence and evaluating it to determine the extent to which the audit criteria are fulfilled.

Audit type Performed by Independence Typical result
First-party (internal) The organization, on itself Lowest — credible to management, not to outsiders Internal report, preparation for later audits
Second-party An interested party, usually a customer auditing a supplier Medium — the customer has an interest in the result Supplier approval or contract decision
Third-party An independent body with no stake in the result (certification body, PCI QSA — Qualified Security Assessor) High Certificate or attestation
Regulatory A public authority (OPC, CAI, CRTC) High, with legal powers Binding orders, penalties (the OPC itself cannot fine; it applies to the Federal Court)
  • An attestation is a formal statement that requirements are fulfilled. A declaration is an attestation issued by the organization about itself; a certification is an attestation issued by a third party.
  • A point-in-time assessment shows the state on the day of the test; continuous monitoring re-evaluates controls automatically and repeatedly between audits.
  • The seven audit principles of ISO 19011: integrity, fair presentation, due professional care, confidentiality, independence, evidence-based approach, risk-based approach.

1.4 The role of conformity assessment in continuous improvement

The PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act) cycle is the management cycle in which the conformity assessment is the Check step. ISO/IEC 27001 (https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) builds it into two clauses:

  • Clause 9 — Performance evaluation: 9.1 monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation; 9.2 internal audit at planned intervals by objective auditors; 9.3 management review by top management.
  • Clause 10 — Improvement: 10.1 continual improvement; 10.2 nonconformity and corrective action (react, find the cause, act, review effectiveness, update, document).
Term What it means Example
Root cause The underlying reason a non-conformity occurred Nobody was ever assigned to the task
Correction Fixes this one instance Encrypt the one unencrypted laptop
Corrective action Removes the cause so it does not come back Enforce encryption for every laptop deployment
Verification of effectiveness A re-test proving the requirement is now fulfilled Re-run the check 30 days later
Historical comparison This year's results compared item by item with last year's Improved, unchanged or regressed
Conformity rate Items conform ÷ items tested, per domain 6 conform out of 8 tested = 75 %

A corrective action is finished only when it has been verified.

2.1 The legal landscape — the hierarchy of obligations

A requirement is a statement of something an organization must do or achieve. Its source is the document it comes from, and its binding force is how strongly it obliges the organization. The hierarchy of obligations goes from the strongest to the weakest:

  1. Laws and regulations — mandatory; a regulation (in the Canadian sense) is a detailed rule adopted by the government under a law and is as mandatory as the law.
  2. Contractual standards — mandatory because a signed contract requires them (PCI DSS through the merchant agreement).
  3. Voluntary standards and frameworks — chosen by the organization (NIST CSF — National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework, CIS Controls, ISO/IEC 27001), unless a law or a contract makes them mandatory.
  4. Internal policies — the organization's own rules.
Body Text it enforces Main powers
OPC — https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/ PIPEDA Investigates, receives breach reports, publishes findings, signs compliance agreements, applies to the Federal Court; it does not fine (offences are prosecuted, fines up to $100,000)
CAI — https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/ Private Sector Act as amended by Law 25 Inspects, investigates, issues orders, imposes AMPs (administrative monetary penalties)
CRTC — https://www.crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/CASL/spam.htm CASL (messages and installation of programs) Investigates and imposes AMPs; the Competition Bureau (https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/) handles misleading messages and the OPC handles address harvesting
Card brands and the acquirer PCI DSS Fees and fines passed on, higher transaction costs, loss of card acceptance
CRA (Canada Revenue Agency) — https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency.html Record keeping under the Income Tax Act Audits, reassessments, penalties
EU supervisory authorities, for example the CNIL (Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés, France) — https://www.cnil.fr/en GDPR Investigations, orders, fines up to €20 million or 4 % of worldwide turnover

Courts sit above all of them. Applicability asks whether a text covers the organization; jurisdiction is the territory and subject matter a law and its regulator cover. The legal (compliance) risk is the possibility of sanctions, loss or reputational damage when an applicable requirement is not met.

2.2 PIPEDA — the federal private-sector privacy law

PIPEDA (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/) Part 1 applies to personal information (information about an identifiable individual) collected, used or disclosed in commercial activities, and to personal information crossing provincial or national borders. Inside Québec, British Columbia and Alberta, a provincial law declared substantially similar applies instead for information handled within the province.

Schedule 1 contains the ten fair-information principles:

No. Principle No. Principle
4.1 Accountability 4.6 Accuracy
4.2 Identifying purposes 4.7 Safeguards
4.3 Consent 4.8 Openness
4.4 Limiting collection 4.9 Individual access
4.5 Limiting use, disclosure and retention 4.10 Challenging compliance
  • Meaningful consent (s. 6.1): the person can understand the nature, purpose and consequences. Express consent is an active agreement; implied consent is reasonably deduced from the situation.
  • Safeguards (4.7): physical, organizational and technological protection matched to the sensitivity of the information.
  • Retention (4.5): keep personal information only as long as necessary, then destroy, erase or anonymize it.

Breaches of security safeguards (mandatory rules since November 1, 2018). A breach is a loss of, unauthorized access to, or unauthorized disclosure of personal information caused by a failure of safeguards. The deciding test is the RROSH (real risk of significant harm), assessed with two factors: the sensitivity of the information and the probability of misuse. Significant harm includes humiliation, damage to reputation, financial loss, identity theft and loss of employment opportunities.

  1. Contain the breach, assess the two factors and document the assessment.
  2. If there is a RROSH: report to the OPC as soon as feasible (s. 10.1(1)) and notify the affected individuals as soon as feasible (s. 10.1(3)).
  3. Notify other organizations that can reduce the harm, such as the police (s. 10.2).
  4. Record every breach, with or without RROSH (s. 10.3), and keep the record 24 months (Breach of Security Safeguards Regulations, SOR/2018-64, s. 6 — https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2018-64/FullText.html).

Knowingly failing to report, notify or keep records is an offence: up to $10,000 on summary conviction, up to $100,000 on indictment.

Up-to-date note — Bill C-36 (proposed, not in force). On June 15, 2026, Bill C-36 was tabled in the House of Commons (https://www.parl.ca/LegisInfo/en/bill/45-1/C-36). It would enact the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, repeal Part 1 of PIPEDA (the rest would become the Electronic Documents Act) and give oversight to a Privacy and Consumer Data Commissioner within a new Digital Safety and Data Protection Commission of Canada. Proposed penalties: AMPs up to the greater of $10 million or 3 % of gross global revenue, and fines up to the greater of $25 million or 5 %. Breach reporting would keep the RROSH test. When this review was prepared (November 17, 2026), LEGISinfo showed the bill at second reading in the House of Commons. PIPEDA remains the law in force, and an auditor always assesses against the law in force.

2.3 Québec's Law 25 — the modernized Private Sector Act

Law 25 (S.Q. 2021, c. 25, formerly Bill 64) modernized the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, chapter P-39.1 (https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1). It applies to every enterprise that collects, holds, uses or communicates personal information in Québec, whatever its size, and it is enforced by the CAI (https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/).

Date in force What started
September 22, 2022 Person in charge of the protection of personal information; confidentiality-incident rules
September 22, 2023 Most obligations: governance policies, PIAs, privacy by default, consent rules, destruction or anonymization, penalties
September 22, 2024 Right to data portability — the law is fully in force
  • Person in charge of the protection of personal information: by default the person with the highest authority (the CEO); the function can be delegated in writing; the title and contact information are published on the website.
  • Governance policies (s. 3.2): published rules on roles, retention, destruction and complaint processing.
  • PIA (privacy impact assessment, s. 3.3): an assessment, before a project, of the privacy risks and the measures that reduce them. Before personal information is communicated outside Québec, a PIA must show adequate protection and a written agreement is required (s. 17).
  • Confidentiality incident (s. 3.6): unauthorized access, use or communication, loss, or any other breach of the protection of personal information. The risk of serious injury is assessed with three factors (s. 3.7): the sensitivity of the information, the anticipated consequences of its use, and the likelihood that it will be used for injurious purposes. When the risk is serious, the CAI and the persons concerned are notified promptly.
  • Register of confidentiality incidents (s. 3.8): every incident is entered, even without risk of serious injury; entries are kept at least 5 years.
  • Privacy by default (s. 9.1): the highest confidentiality settings apply without any action by the person.
  • Data portability (s. 27): computerized personal information is provided in a structured, commonly used technological format on request.
  • Destruction or anonymization (s. 23): when the purposes are achieved, unless a law requires a longer preservation period. Sensitive information requires express consent.
Penalty Who decides Maximum for an enterprise
AMP CAI $10 million or 2 % of worldwide turnover, whichever is greater
Penal fine A court From $15,000 to $25 million or 4 % of worldwide turnover (doubled for a repeat offence)
Punitive damages A court, in a civil action At least $1,000 for an intentional infringement or gross fault

2.4 The GDPR — why a European law applies to a Montréal retailer

The GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) protects personal data (information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, the data subject). The controller decides the purposes and means of the processing; the processor processes on the controller's behalf.

Territorial scope — Article 3(2). The GDPR applies to organizations outside the EU that offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor their behaviour there. Érable Cycles sells online to customers in the EU, so the GDPR applies to their data. A law that reaches beyond the territory that adopted it has an extraterritorial effect.

Article What it asks
Art. 5 — principles Lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; plus accountability (Art. 5(2))
Art. 6 — lawful bases Consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests
Art. 27 — EU representative A person established in the EU, designated in writing by a non-EU controller
Art. 30 — ROPA Record of processing activities: purposes, data, recipients, transfers, retention, security
Art. 33 — notification Notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach
Art. 34 — communication Inform the data subjects without undue delay when the breach is likely to result in a high risk to them
Art. 35 — DPIA Data protection impact assessment before high-risk processing

Fines (Art. 83) have two levels, whichever amount is higher: up to €10 million or 2 % of worldwide annual turnover (for example records, security, breach notification, DPIA, representative), and up to €20 million or 4 % (for example the Art. 5 principles, lawful bases, rights of data subjects, transfers). Transfers from the EU to Canada are simpler than to many countries, because the European Commission recognizes Canada as adequate for organizations subject to PIPEDA (https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/adequacy-decisions_en), a status confirmed in its January 2024 review. The EDPB (European Data Protection Board, https://www.edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/the-european-data-protection-board_en) brings the national authorities together.

2.5 PCI DSS v4.0.1 — the contractual standard for card payments

PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) is published by the PCI SSC (Payment Card Industry Security Standards Council, https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/). It is not a law: it becomes mandatory for a merchant through the merchant agreement with its acquirer, the bank that processes its card payments.

  • Account data = cardholder data (PAN — primary account number, cardholder name, expiration date, service code) + SAD (sensitive authentication data: track data, card verification code, PIN — personal identification number — data). SAD is never stored after authorization.
  • The CDE (cardholder data environment) is the people, processes and technologies that store, process or transmit cardholder data or SAD. The scope is the CDE plus every system connected to it or able to affect its security. Segmentation isolates other systems and keeps them out of scope.
Goal Requirements (summarized in our own words)
Build and maintain a secure network and systems 1 Network security controls · 2 Secure configurations
Protect account data 3 Protect stored account data · 4 Strong cryptography over open, public networks
Maintain a vulnerability management program 5 Protection against malicious software · 6 Secure systems and software
Implement strong access control measures 7 Access by business need to know · 8 Identify users and authenticate access · 9 Restrict physical access
Regularly monitor and test networks 10 Log and monitor all access · 11 Test security regularly
Maintain an information security policy 12 Organizational policies and programs

Validation. Every year the merchant validates its compliance in the form the acquirer requires. The largest merchants receive a ROC (Report on Compliance) written by a QSA (Qualified Security Assessor). Smaller merchants complete an SAQ and sign an AOC (Attestation of Compliance); some SAQs also require quarterly external scans by an ASV (Approved Scanning Vendor).

SAQ For merchants that…
A Accept cards only remotely and fully outsource all account-data functions; online, the whole payment page comes from the provider (since January 2025 the merchant also confirms its site is not open to script attacks)
A-EP Run an e-commerce site that does not receive card data but controls the redirection to the payment page
B / B-IP Use only standalone terminals (dial-out or IP-connected); no electronic storage
C-VT / C Key transactions into a provider's web-based virtual terminal / use payment applications connected to the Internet
P2PE Use only hardware terminals of a validated, PCI-listed P2PE solution; no electronic storage
D Do not qualify for any other SAQ

At Érable Cycles the hosted payment page keeps card numbers off the web store, and the P2PE terminals encrypt in the device: an SAQ A-type validation for the web store and SAQ P2PE for the stores. No card number is stored, and the company must still be able to prove it.

2.6 CASL — Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation and the newsletter

CASL (https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/; government information: https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en) governs the CEM (commercial electronic message): an electronic message with a commercial purpose sent to an electronic address.

  • Three requirements of every CEM (s. 6): consent, identification of the sender with contact information, and an unsubscribe mechanism.
  • Express consent: active and informed; it has no expiry date and lasts until withdrawn; a pre-checked box is never valid.
  • Implied consent: deduced from a situation listed in the law, mainly an existing business relationship — a purchase in the past 2 years or an inquiry in the past 6 months.
  • Proof of consent (s. 13): the sender must be able to prove the consent it relies on.
  • Unsubscribe mechanism: free, valid for at least 60 days after the message, and the request is given effect within 10 business days.
  • Installing a computer program on another person's device in a commercial activity requires express consent (s. 8).
  • Enforcement: the CRTC (messages and programs), the Competition Bureau (false or misleading messages), the OPC (harvesting of addresses). Maximum AMP per violation: $1 million for an individual, $10 million for a business. Proceedings must start within three years, which is one reason consent records are kept.
  • Recent figures: from April 1 to September 30, 2025, the Spam Reporting Centre received 152,603 complaints; the CRTC issued 153 notices to produce, 123 warning letters and one notice of violation with a $50,000 penalty.

2.7 Mention only — United States state laws and CRA record keeping

The United States has no single federal privacy law for retailers; state laws such as the CCPA (California Consumer Privacy Act), enforced by the California Privacy Protection Agency (https://privacy.ca.gov/about-us/), each have their own thresholds (comparison table of the IAPP — International Association of Privacy Professionals: https://iapp.org/resources/article/state-comparison-table). The CRA requires books and records to be kept six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to — a legal minimum (https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html).

3.1 NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 — Functions, Tiers and Profiles

The NIST CSF (National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework, https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) is a voluntary, free framework published by NIST, an agency of the United States Department of Commerce. Version 2.0 was published on February 26, 2024 as NIST CSWP 29 (https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/CSWP/NIST.CSWP.29.pdf). Version 1.0 (2014) targeted critical infrastructure; version 2.0 is written for organizations of any size. The CSF describes outcomes — what a security program should achieve — not products and not configuration settings.

It has three components:

  • The Core — six Functions, 22 Categories and 106 Subcategories (broad outcome → group of outcomes → one specific, testable outcome). The Functions are Govern (GV), Identify (ID), Protect (PR), Detect (DE), Respond (RS) and Recover (RC). Govern is new in 2.0: strategy, expectations, policy, roles, oversight and supply-chain risk.
  • The Tiers — four descriptions of how rigorously cybersecurity risk is governed and managed: Tier 1 Partial, Tier 2 Risk Informed, Tier 3 Repeatable, Tier 4 Adaptive. They are not maturity levels to climb for their own sake; Tier 2 moving towards Tier 3 is realistic for an SME (small or medium-sized enterprise) such as Érable Cycles.
  • The Organizational Profiles — the Current Profile records the outcomes achieved today; the Target Profile records the outcomes wanted. The difference is the gap, and the gap becomes the action plan. A Profile has a requirement (the Subcategory), a statement of the actual situation, a comparison and an action — which is why it is called a small conformity assessment.

Informative References map each Subcategory to detailed sources (NIST SP — Special Publication — 800-53 Rev. 5, CIS Controls, ISO/IEC 27001) through the NIST OLIR (Online Informative References) program and the CPRT (Cybersecurity and Privacy Reference Tool, https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/cprt). For a small company, the recommended entry point is NIST SP 1300, the Small Business Quick-Start Guide (https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber).

What the CSF does not give: exact configuration values (those come from CIS Benchmarks and vendor baselines) and certification (nobody is "CSF-certified").

When does an SME choose ISO/IEC 27001? When a customer, a partner or a tender requires the certificate, when it sells into markets that expect it, or when it needs an external signal of trust. A first certificate commonly takes 12 to 18 months of work. The standard is a licensed document: we cite control numbers and never copy its text.

Choosing frameworks for an SME — the logic behind the three-sentence justification:

  1. PCI DSS is not a choice. As soon as cards are accepted, it applies by contract; the only decision is the scope.
  2. Start with CIS Controls v8.1 IG1 for concrete safeguards: 56 actions, free, prioritized, with Benchmarks and official mappings.
  3. Add a NIST CSF 2.0 Organizational Profile for structure and reporting to the CEO, year after year.
  4. Move to ISO/IEC 27001 only when a customer, a partner or a market requires the certificate.

3.4 Data retention policies — legal minimums, legal maximums and the retention schedule

Two forces pull in opposite directions. A legal minimum says "keep at least this long" (tax records, PCI DSS logs, breach registers). A legal maximum says "keep no longer than necessary" (privacy laws). The retention period is counted from a trigger: the event that starts the clock (end of the tax year, account closure, last activity, recording date).

Source Rule Minimum or maximum
CRA and Revenu Québec (https://www.revenuquebec.ca/en/) Books and supporting documents kept 6 years from the end of the last tax year Minimum
PIPEDA principle 4.5 Retain personal information only as long as necessary; destroy, erase or anonymize Maximum
PIPEDA Breach Regulations (SOR/2018-64) s. 6 Record of every breach kept 24 months Minimum
Law 25, s. 23 Destroy or anonymize when the purposes are achieved, unless a law requires longer Maximum
Law 25 — incident regulation Register entries kept 5 years after awareness of the incident Minimum
GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) — storage limitation Personal data kept in identifiable form no longer than necessary Maximum
PCI DSS v4.0.1 req. 3.2.1 Account data kept to a minimum; quarterly check that expired data is gone Maximum, with a verification duty
PCI DSS v4.0.1 req. 10.5.1 Audit log history kept at least 12 months, the last 3 months immediately available Minimum
CASL s. 13 Proof of consent kept for the life of the consent plus the 3-year limitation period Minimum

When a minimum and a maximum collide (an invoice is both a tax record and personal information), the legal minimum applies first, because the privacy laws make an exception for periods required by another law; after it, the maximum applies again.

A usable retention schedule has seven columns: record type (with PI and CHD yes/no), legal basis (minimum and maximum), retention period, trigger, disposal method, owner (a business role, not the IT custodian) and exceptions. The exception column holds the legal hold: when a dispute, a complaint or an investigation is reasonably anticipated, destruction is suspended for the records concerned. Anonymization is an irreversible transformation after which the person can no longer be identified; under Law 25 it follows a 2024 regulation and is an alternative to destruction.

A schedule check finds two kinds of non-conformity: destroyed too early (below a legal minimum: tax records shredded after three years, logs rotated after 30 days when 12 months are required) and kept too long (past the purpose without legal basis: a newsletter list with addresses collected ten years ago, camera footage kept for a year).

4.3 Critical assets and their owners

A critical asset is one whose loss, corruption or disclosure would stop or seriously damage a business process the organization cannot do without. Criticality is a relation between an asset and a process, found with a BIA (business impact analysis): list the business processes, rate the impact of their interruption, list the assets each process depends on, and let each asset inherit the impact of the processes it supports. Price, age or data volume do not make an asset critical.

A single point of failure (SPOF) is a component whose failure stops a process because no alternative exists: Érable Cycles has one domain controller, one firewall per site, one EC2 instance with one RDS database for the web store, and one payment provider. Redundancy removes the SPOF without changing the impact of the process. The dependencies column records these links in both directions.

Every asset names two roles: the owner (the business role accountable — the E-commerce Manager owns the web store database even though Amazon runs the hardware) and the custodian (who operates and protects it according to the owner's decisions). The user uses the asset for an approved purpose and is not recorded in the inventory. Two frequent mistakes: making IT the owner of everything, and leaving the owner empty for supplier services (the owner of the payment provider relationship is the manager who signed the contract). An empty owner cell is a finding in itself. Lab 2 Part A asks for five critical assets with one sentence of rationale each; with the two VMs, they become the scope of the assessment plan.

5.1 Audit methods and techniques

Audit criteria are the requirements used as the reference (laws, contracts, standards, policies). Audit evidence is verifiable information relevant to a criterion. An audit method is the defined way the auditor collects evidence. Comparing evidence with criteria gives an audit finding (conformity, nonconformity or observation). A finding without evidence is only an opinion.

Method What the auditor does Question it answers Érable Cycles example
Document review Reads policies, procedures, records What is supposed to happen? Is the Patch Management Procedure approved and dated?
Interview Asks the people who perform the activity What do people say happens? The IT Administrator explains how critical patches are approved
Walkthrough Follows one real case from start to end with the person who performs it What happens in practice? One new-employee request, from the HR (Human Resources) form to the AD (Active Directory) account
Observation Watches an activity or a setting as it happens What happens in practice? The nightly backup job starts and ends successfully
Sampling Tests a subset of a population with a stated rule Which items were checked, and why? 10 of the 45 laptops checked for patch level
Re-performance Runs the check or the tool personally Does the control give the expected result? Get-HotFix run by the auditor on LAB-WIN11

Three references, one vocabulary. NIST SP 800-53A Rev. 5 (https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/a/r5/final) uses three assessment methods — examine (documents, configurations, records), interview (people) and test (exercise a mechanism) — with two attributes: depth (how rigorous) and coverage (how many objects). The PCI DSS testing procedures use the verbs examine, observe and interview. ISO 19011:2026, the fourth edition published in May 2026 (https://www.iso.org/standard/88984.html), describes document review, interviews, observation and sampling, and adds guidance on remote auditing methods (video calls, screen sharing, shared document stores).

Sampling. The population is every item a requirement applies to (the 45 laptops); the sample is the part actually tested. Judgement-based sampling picks items with knowledge of risk (laptops with administrator rights) — efficient, but not statistically generalizable. Statistical sampling gives every item a known chance (random selection), so the result can be extended with a measured confidence. A sampling plan records the population, method, size, selection rule and reason for each pick; a random generator with a fixed seed gives the same picks every time, so another auditor can verify them. Sampling risk is the risk that the sample leads to a different conclusion than testing 100 %. PCI DSS requires either 100 % testing or a defined sampling method that covers every variant of the population.

Evidence quality. Reliability increases when evidence comes from an independent source, is an original, is generated by the system rather than typed by a person, and is obtained directly by the auditor. Re-performance is therefore stronger than an interview. Corroboration confirms one source with a second, independent source (the administrator says the firewall is on; the auditor runs Get-NetFirewallProfile). The ISO 19011 audit life cycle has six stages: initiate, prepare, conduct, report, complete, follow up — the plan is "prepare", Lab 3 is "conduct", the Project is "report" and "follow up".

5.2 Selection and application of appropriate tests

A test compares an actual state with an expected result for one requirement. Before execution it has five parts: requirement, method, sample, expected result (pass rule) and evidence to keep. After execution the auditor adds the actual result, the verdict (PASS, FAIL, N/A — not applicable — with a reason, or Not tested with a reason) and the evidence reference. A test is repeatable when another auditor following it gets the same result.

The requirement says… First method Corroborate with
documented / approved / published Document review Interview of the owner
performed / reviewed every N months Review of records over the period + sampling Interview
configured / enabled / disabled Re-performance on the system Baseline document
personnel are aware Interviews Observation
only authorized ports are reachable Reachability test (Nmap) Firewall-rule review

Automated tests (CIS-CAT Lite, Lynis, scripts) are fast, repeatable and check 100 % of the settings they know, but they can produce a false positive (a reported problem that does not exist) or a false negative (a real problem missed), and every tool has limited coverage — CIS-CAT Lite assesses Windows 10 and 11 Enterprise, Ubuntu 20.04 and Google Chrome, so Lab 3 uses Lynis on Ubuntu 24.04. Manual tests remain necessary for policies, people and physical controls.

A test of design checks that a control, as described, would meet the requirement. A test of operating effectiveness checks that it actually operated over a period. One screenshot proves a setting at one moment, not operation over time.

The expected result must be measurable: "patching is good" cannot be tested; "the newest security update was installed at most 30 days before the test date" can (it follows PCI DSS 6.3.3). Evidence handling: one naming rule (L3-E07_MC-03_hotfix_LAB-WIN11.csv), the collection date, a SHA-256 fingerprint (64 hexadecimal characters that change if one byte changes), masking of real personal information, one line per file in the evidence index. Test once, satisfy many: the same patch report supports CIS 7.3, ISO/IEC 27001 A.8.8, PCI DSS 6.3.3 and CSF PR.PS-02.

5.3 Development of audit checklists aligned to frameworks

An audit checklist lists the items to verify with what is needed to test each one. It makes the assessment complete, consistent and repeatable, and plans the evidence in advance. ISO 19011 calls such documents work documents; ISO/IEC TS 27008:2019 (https://www.iso.org/standard/67397.html) gives guidance on assessing security controls. A problem found outside the list is recorded as an observation.

Anatomy of a checklist line: ID and name; type (C configuration, T technical control, A administrative control, S segmentation or access); framework references (CIS Safeguard with its IG, ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A control, PCI DSS requirement, CSF Subcategory); the requirement in our own words; test procedure; method; sample; expected result; expected evidence; then result and notes. When an item has no equivalent in a framework, write "-" (MC-11, disk encryption on laptops, has no PCI DSS reference because no cardholder data is on the laptops). Tools such as CIS CSAT and CISO Assistant (https://github.com/intuitem/ciso-assistant-community) manage checklists with mappings.

5.4 Risk analysis fundamentals for assessing impact and likelihood

ISO 31000:2018 (https://www.iso.org/standard/65694.html) defines risk as the effect of uncertainty on objectives; in practice, the combination of likelihood and impact of an event. The event happens when a threat (a potential cause of an incident) exploits a vulnerability (a weakness). Inherent risk is the risk before controls; residual risk remains after controls or treatment. A compensating control is a different control that reduces the same risk when the expected control is missing (a firewall rule limiting SSH — Secure Shell — to the administration subnet).

Approach How risk is expressed
Qualitative Ordered levels in words (Low, Medium, High)
Semi-quantitative Numbers given to the levels (1, 2, 3), usually multiplied
Quantitative Money: SLE (single loss expectancy) = asset value × exposure factor; ARO (annualized rate of occurrence) = events per year; ALE (annualized loss expectancy) = SLE × ARO

Example: one day of online sales is worth $12,000; an outage loses half a day (exposure factor 0.5), so SLE = $6,000; one outage every two years gives ARO = 0.5, so ALE = $3,000 per year. NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 (https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final) uses five levels (Very Low to Very High) and the same three approaches. The course uses a semi-quantitative 3×3 matrix:

Level Likelihood — one-line definition Impact — one-line definition
1 Low Needs physical access or several unlikely conditions, or a strong compensating control exists Inconvenience only; no legal duty affected
2 Medium Needs internal network access or a specific condition, or only a partial compensating control A process degraded more than one day with a workaround, or internal data exposed
3 High Reachable from the Internet or by any employee, already observed or exploited, no compensating control A legal duty breached (PIPEDA, Law 25, PCI DSS) or a High-impact process stopped

Score-to-level rule: likelihood × impact → 1–2 Low, 3–4 Medium, 6 High, 9 Critical.

A finding is rated only when it is well described with four elements — condition (what was observed), criterion (the requirement), cause (why the gap exists) and effect (what can happen). The rating follows five steps: (1) find the asset and its business impact in the inventory; (2) choose the likelihood, counting exposure and compensating controls; (3) choose the impact; (4) compute score and level; (5) write a one-sentence justification quoting the scale. Calibration = several auditors rate the same findings and compare. When two findings have the same score, the one with a legal or contractual deadline goes first.

Risk is used before testing (risk-based planning: critical assets first, with more depth and coverage) and after testing (every nonconformity is rated). ISO/IEC 27005:2022 names four risk treatment options: modification (add or improve controls), retention (accept with a documented decision), avoidance (stop the activity) and sharing (insurance, contract). The auditor rates and reports; the risk owner (usually the asset owner) decides.

5.5 Scheduling and planning compliance assessment activities

ISO 19011 separates the audit programme (arrangements for one or more audits over a period toward a purpose — for example access control in the first quarter, backups in the second) from the audit plan (activities and arrangements of one audit). ISO/IEC 27001 clause 9.2.2 asks for an internal audit programme with frequency, methods, responsibilities, criteria, scope, objective auditors and reporting to management.

An audit plan states objectives, scope, criteria, methods and samples, schedule, roles, resources, communication rules, report format and confidentiality rules. The Lab 2 Part B plan has six sections: (1) scope and objectives, (2) mandate and authorization, (3) audit checklist, (4) tests, samples and evidence, (5) risk method, (6) schedule, tools and meetings.

The mandate comes before any test. A mandate is the written authorization from the person with authority over the systems (asset owner or chief executive) that defines systems, activities, exclusions, period and contact. Without authorization, accessing a computer system can be a criminal offence in Canada (Criminal Code, s. 342.1 — https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-46/section-342.1.html). ISO/IEC 27002:2022 control 8.34 asks that audit tests be agreed with management, read-only whenever possible, run outside business hours if they may affect availability, and logged. NIST SP 800-115 (https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/115/final) gives a rules of engagement template.

Four kinds of constraints: business hours (stores and head office open 09:00–18:00, Monday to Saturday — scans after 18:00); the change freeze (November 20 to 30: Black Friday on November 27, Cyber Monday on November 30 — no change and no intrusive test); dependencies (baseline tools of Part A first, scripts of Part B second, controls of Part C last); preconditions (mandate signed, snapshots of both VMs before the first test).

The opening meeting (before the first test) confirms scope, objectives and schedule, the mandate, constraints and contacts, and how evidence and preliminary findings will be communicated. The closing meeting (after the tests) presents the findings with their ratings, confirms the facts and notes disagreements, and agrees on the dates of the report and of the corrective-action plan.

5.6 From plan to evidence

In Lab 3 every checklist item becomes a test record: the procedure is executed, the evidence saved with the naming rule and fingerprinted, a line added to the evidence index, the result recorded; every nonconformity becomes a finding (condition, criterion, cause, effect) rated with the matrix. Traceability is the ability to follow an item from the requirement to the asset, the test, the evidence and the finding. Common mistakes that lose points: an unsigned or undated mandate, items without framework references, a pass rule that is not measurable ("firewall OK"), a sample without size or reason, a scan planned during business hours or after November 20, asset IDs that change between Part A and Part B, copied ISO text or real personal information.

Exam traps — Chapter 5 - Walkthrough (one real case end to end) ≠ observation (watching an activity) ≠ re-performance (the auditor runs it). - Judgement-based samples cannot be generalized statistically; a seed makes random picks reproducible. - Test of design (would it work?) ≠ test of operating effectiveness (did it work over time?). - 1–2 Low, 3–4 Medium, 6 High, 9 Critical — there is no score of 5, 7 or 8 in a 3×3 matrix. - Audit programme (many audits over a period) ≠ audit plan (one audit).

From this chapter on, the course moves from the plan to the evidence. A configuration audit compares the real settings of a system with an approved baseline, using tools on Windows, Linux and in the cloud.

7.1 What a configuration audit is

A configuration is the full set of settings that decides how a system behaves. Vendors ship default settings chosen for compatibility and ease of use, not for a company's policy (on a stand-alone Windows 11 machine the local "Minimum password length" is usually 0). Hardening changes settings to reduce the attack surface; it is done by administrators. A configuration audit is done afterwards by the auditor, to verify with evidence that the hardening exists and is still in place.

A configuration audit is a structured comparison of the actual settings of a system with the expected settings of an approved security configuration baseline. It produces three things: one verdict per setting (conform, non-conform, or not applicable with a reason), a conformity score, and a list of findings with their evidence. NIST SP 800-128 (https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/128/upd1/final) describes a baseline as a documented set of specifications, reviewed and agreed at a point in time, that can be changed only through change control: the baseline must exist in writing before the audit, and every change must leave a trace.

Configuration drift is the movement of the actual settings away from the baseline after the system was configured correctly (a firewall profile switched off for troubleshooting and forgotten, an update resetting a value). Comparing two runs shows three kinds of change: improved (FAIL → PASS), regressed (PASS → FAIL) and unchanged. Drift is controlled by change management (approve before) and configuration monitoring (detect after); a regressed setting with no change ticket is a finding in itself.

  • Authenticated access is needed: settings are read from inside the system with administrator or root rights; an external scan cannot see a password policy.
  • Agent-based collection uses software installed on each host that reports regularly (Configuration Manager client, Intune, Wazuh agent); agentless collection runs a tool temporarily or reads the configuration remotely (CIS-CAT run locally, SSH-based checks, cloud APIs — application programming interfaces).
  • Scope: operating systems, applications, network devices and cloud services.
Framework Reference What it requires (own words)
CIS Controls v8.1 Safeguard 4.1 (IG1) A documented secure configuration process, reviewed regularly
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.8.9 Configuration management Configurations established, documented, implemented, monitored, reviewed
PCI DSS v4.0.1 2.2.1 Configuration standards for all components, following industry-accepted hardening standards
NIST CSF 2.0 PR.PS-01 Configuration management practices established and applied

Two kinds of scores. The conformity score = conform items ÷ items tested × 100 (5 of 12 = 41.7 %); CIS-CAT computes its score this way. The Lynis hardening index adds points awarded by tests and scales them from 0 to 100 — it is not a percentage of passed tests. Neither number replaces the list of findings.

7.2 Baselines — where the expected values come from

Published baselines exist because no organization can decide thousands of values alone, and an auditor cannot defend a value nobody recognizes. PCI DSS 2.2.1 asks that configuration standards follow industry-accepted hardening standards or vendor recommendations.

  • CIS Benchmarks (https://www.cisecurity.org/cis-benchmarks): consensus-based guides for more than 100 technologies, free for non-commercial use. Each recommendation has a number and title (for example 1.1.4 minimum password length of 14 or more), a profile, an assessment status (Automated when a tool can check it, Manual when a person must), rationale, impact, audit procedure, remediation, default value and CIS Controls mapping. Level 1 = essential settings with little impact on daily use; Level 2 = defence in depth that may reduce functionality. Versions matter: in November 2026 CIS-CAT Lite contains CIS Microsoft Windows 11 Enterprise v5.1.0, Windows 10 Enterprise v5.0.0, Windows 10 Stand-alone v5.0.0, Google Chrome v1.1.0 and Ubuntu Linux 20.04 LTS (Long-Term Support) v3.0.0. A test record always states the benchmark version.
  • Microsoft security baselines and the SCT (Security Compliance Toolkit, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/download/details.aspx?id=55319): GPO (Group Policy Object) backups, a documentation spreadsheet, Policy Analyzer and LGPO (Local Group Policy Object utility). The page updated October 8, 2026 offers the Windows 11 v26H2 Security Baseline v2 and baselines for 25H2, 24H2, 23H2, Windows Server 2025 and 2022, Microsoft Edge and Microsoft 365 Apps. Guide: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/security/operating-system-security/device-management/windows-security-configuration-framework/windows-security-baselines.
  • Vendor guides: Canonical's USG (Ubuntu Security Guide, https://ubuntu.com/security/certifications/docs/usg) applies and audits CIS and DISA-STIG profiles with an Ubuntu Pro subscription (free for personal use on up to 5 machines); Netgate documents pfSense hardening.
  • DISA STIGs (Defense Information Systems Agency Security Technical Implementation Guides, https://www.cyber.mil/stigs): configuration standards of the U.S. Department of Defense, stricter than CIS Level 1.
  • SCAP (Security Content Automation Protocol, https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/security-content-automation-protocol): NIST formats that make checklists machine-readable — XCCDF (Extensible Configuration Checklist Description Format) describes the checklist, OVAL (Open Vulnerability and Assessment Language) describes how to test each item. The NCP (National Checklist Program, https://ncp.nist.gov/, SP 800-70) publishes such checklists.
  • Cloud benchmarks: CIS AWS Foundations Benchmark (v5.0.0 supported by AWS Security Hub CSPM since October 16, 2025) and CIS Microsoft Azure Foundations Benchmark.

Five written rules for choosing a baseline: (1) the official baseline for the exact product and version; (2) the profile that matches the role and risk (Level 1 for Érable Cycles); (3) a tool can measure it, otherwise plan a manual procedure; (4) record name, version and profile in every test record; (5) when two baselines disagree, the stricter value wins unless the risk owner documents an exception — tailoring. Example: account lockout threshold — CIS Level 1 asks for 5 or fewer (not 0), Microsoft uses 10; lockout duration — CIS 15 minutes or more, Microsoft 10. The course keeps 5 attempts and 15 minutes.

7.3 Windows tools — CIS-CAT Lite and Policy Analyzer (MC-01)

CIS-CAT Lite is the free edition of the CIS Configuration Assessment Tool: the same engine as CIS-CAT Pro Assessor v4 (current release v4.68.0 of November 2, 2026), but a limited set of benchmarks, the graphical interface only and an HTML report only (documentation: https://ciscat-assessor.docs.cisecurity.org/). It reads the XCCDF and OVAL content, runs each Automated check locally with administrator rights and records pass, fail, error or unknown. CIS-CAT score = passed ÷ (passed + failed) scored recommendations × 100; Manual recommendations are listed but not scored.

Running it on LAB-WIN11: snapshot "before-lab3" and authorization letter in force; Administrator Command Prompt in the extracted folder (never inside the ZIP); Assessor-GUI.bat; benchmark Windows 11 Enterprise v5.1.0, profile Level 1, HTML report; record the score and five failed recommendations; rename the report L3-E01_MC-01_ciscat-report_LAB-WIN11.html. Policy Analyzer compares sets of Group Policy settings and shows differences, conflicts and redundancies. 7.4 Linux tools — Lynis, OpenSCAP and the Ubuntu Security Guide (MC-02)

Lynis (https://cisofy.com/lynis/) is an open-source security auditing tool for Linux and Unix by the Dutch company CISOfy, under the GPL (GNU General Public License) v3. It runs locally with root rights, needs no agent and no network connection, and performs a few hundred tests grouped in categories with prefixes (AUTH, BOOT, KRNL, PKGS, NETW, FIRE, SSH, LOGG, FILE, FINT, HRDN, BANN, ACCT…), each with an identifier such as AUTH-9286. Lynis is not a CIS Benchmark tool: it uses its own tests.

  • sudo apt install -y lynis gives version 3.0.9 on Ubuntu 24.04 (old; 3.1.x is current); sudo lynis audit system runs the audit.
  • Two files: /var/log/lynis.log (every step) and /var/log/lynis-report.dat (machine-readable key=value lines — the evidence file).
  • Hardening index (0–100, from test points, not a percentage); warnings (warning[]= — important problems, handled first, for example PKGS-7392 vulnerable packages); suggestions (suggestion[]= — improvements, for example AUTH-9286 minimum password age in /etc/login.defs).
  • Reading order: warnings first, then suggestions grouped by category and ranked by risk. The auditor records; the administrator fixes.

MC-02 pass rule: hardening index ≥ 70 and zero warnings. The course sample (Lynis 3.0.9 on Ubuntu 24.04.5 LTS): index 57, 3 warnings, 45 suggestions — FAIL.

Alternatives that follow CIS profiles: OpenSCAP (https://www.open-scap.org/), a NIST-validated SCAP scanner using ComplianceAsCode content (oscap xccdf eval --profile … --report report.html …); USG (sudo usg audit cis_level1_server with Ubuntu Pro); Wazuh (https://wazuh.com/), which checks CIS policies continuously with its Security Configuration Assessment module. The course uses Lynis because CIS-CAT Lite covers only Ubuntu 20.04 and Lynis runs on any release without a subscription.

8.1 Scripts that automate configuration checks — Windows and PowerShell

Checking ten settings by hand on 45 laptops means about 450 screens to open, read and copy, with copy errors and skipped screens. A check script reads a list of settings and compares each one with the expected value. It is useful to an auditor because it is repeatable (the same system gives the same result, so a colleague can re-perform it), consistent (every host is judged with the same rule) and it produces evidence (observed value, rule and verdict written to a file that can be fingerprinted). A script does not replace judgement: a wrong rule produces wrong verdicts very fast, so rules are written in the plan first and copied into the script second.

The four-part pattern of every check (also the columns of the evidence file and of the findings table):

Part Meaning Example — W04 account lockout
Check What is tested and where the value is read Lockout policy, read from the local security policy
Expected value The pass rule (≥, ≤, between, equals, one of) Threshold 1–5 AND duration ≥ 15 minutes (CIS 1.2.1, 1.2.2)
Observed value What the system returns, copied without interpretation Threshold 10, duration 10 minutes
Verdict PASS, FAIL, N/A with a reason, NOT TESTED with a reason FAIL

The pass rule must be measurable ("length ≥ 14", not "strong passwords"). When a rule combines conditions with AND, one failed condition makes the whole check FAIL.

PowerShell basics. Commands are cmdlets named Verb-Noun (Get-HotFix, Get-NetFirewallProfile, Get-FileHash); variables start with $; the pipeline | passes objects with named properties (Bash passes plain text). A check script has five parts: parameters (for example -OutDir C:\Lab3), collection with read-only commands (secedit /export, registry reads, Get-HotFix), comparison (one if per check), recording (a function that adds one row per check; Export-Csv writes the file) and fingerprint (Get-FileHash -Algorithm SHA256).

Running a downloaded script safely:

  1. Verify the fingerprint: Get-FileHash must print the same SHA-256 value as SHA256SUMS.txt; a different value means the file was changed — do not run it.
  2. Allow scripts for this window only: Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process -ExecutionPolicy Bypass; the change disappears when the window closes. Microsoft describes the execution policy as a safety feature, not a security boundary (https://learn.microsoft.com/powershell/module/microsoft.powershell.core/about/about_execution_policies).
  3. Unblock the verified file: Unblock-File removes the mark Windows puts on downloaded files.
  4. Run as administrator, read-only: the kit scripts only read; changing a setting needs the owner's approval, a test and a way back.

Both come from the measurement, not the system: wrong source, old data, unexpected format, missing rights. Examples: W05 reports "screen lock not configured" because a registry value is absent, while Intune sets the lock by device policy (possible false positive); L02 reports 0 updates waiting from 9-day-old package lists (possible false negative).

Corroboration confirms a doubtful result with a second, independent source (the Intune policy report or a timed idle test for W05; sudo apt update and re-performance for L02). A compensating control reduces the same risk when the required control is missing — root may still sign in to WEB-APP01 over SSH with a key, but SSH is reachable only from the administration VPN (virtual private network): the verdict stays Non-conform, the compensating control is written in the note and may lower the likelihood. PCI DSS v4.0.1 (Appendix B) accepts a compensating control only when it meets the intent of the original requirement, gives a similar level of defence and goes beyond the other requirements already in place.

High risk first: after triage (usable, re-test, corroborate, correct), confirmed failures are handled by risk with the Lab 2 matrix. Vulnerability management also uses the CVSS (Common Vulnerability Scoring System, https://www.first.org/cvss/) and the CISA KEV (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency Known Exploited Vulnerabilities) catalog (https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog); for configuration findings the course matrix is enough.

Term Definition Érable Cycles example
Non-conformity A requirement not met (a fact found by the audit) F-05: automatic security updates off on WEB-APP01
Deviation A recorded difference between required and actual state, tracked until closed F-05 written in the deviation register with an owner

An exception does not make a system conform. The finding stays Non-conform with the mention "exception approved until 2026-12-01 (EX-01)". What changes is that the gap is known, owned, limited in time and controlled.

The eight elements of a valid exception (all eight, or the request is rejected): (1) finding and requirement identified; (2) a specific business reason, not convenience; (3) a compensating control; (4) risk rated before and after — a Critical residual risk is never accepted in this course; (5) an owner; (6) an approver with authority who is not the requester (independence — ISO/IEC 27001 clause 6.1.3 asks risk owners to approve treatment and accept residual risk); (7) an end date, at most 90 days; (8) a review date.

At Érable Cycles the approver is the CEO, who is also the person in charge of personal information under Law 25; the IT Administrator can request or recommend, but cannot approve an exception for his own systems. At the end date there are only two outcomes: the requirement is met and verified, or a new request goes through the eight elements; an expired exception is reported as an open non-conformity. The deviation register lists every request, approved or rejected (rejected requests stay, with the missing elements).

8.6 Closing the loop — Check → Act and verification of the fix

In PDCA, the Lab 2 plan is Plan, Érable Cycles running its systems is Do, the check scripts and findings table are Check, and the corrective actions taken by the organization are Act. ISO/IEC 27001 clause 10.2 asks to correct a nonconformity and then review the effectiveness of the corrective action — which means running the check again. In a real audit the auditor recommends and the organization corrects; an auditor who fixes what he audits loses independence.

Remediate safely under change control: evidence first (everything saved and fingerprinted; original FAIL rows never overwritten); a new snapshot before-fix as the backout plan; one ticket that names exactly which checks it should fix; the smallest correct change (net accounts /lockoutthreshold:5 /lockoutduration:15 /lockoutwindow:15 fixes W04; sudo ufw allow 22/tcp && sudo ufw enable fixes L08 — allowing SSH before enabling the firewall keeps your own access).

Re-run, compare, verify. Four outcomes per check: fixed (FAIL → PASS), unchanged, regressed (PASS → FAIL — the fix broke something), unplanned change (a check the ticket did not mention changed). The change is verified only when every planned check is fixed and nothing else moved; the comparison is evidence E13. Choose fixes that the check can confirm: net accounts /minpwlen:14 /uniquepw:24 sets length and history, but complexity can only be turned on in the Local Security Policy or a group policy, so W03 would stay FAIL.

Act for the whole organization: fixing one VM proves the fix works; it does not fix 45 laptops. The real corrective action changes the baseline that configures every laptop (a group policy on SRV-DC01 or an Intune profile), then re-tests a sample — for example re-running the scripts 30 days later in the follow-up plan.

Exam traps — Chapter 8 - The script collects and compares; the auditor confirms and interprets. - N/A and NOT TESTED always need a reason; PASS needs none. - A false negative (wrong PASS) is more dangerous than a false positive (wrong FAIL). - A compensating control or an approved exception never turns Non-conform into Conform. - Verified = every planned check fixed and nothing else moved.

9.1 Controls and how to verify them

A security control is a safeguard or countermeasure that protects the confidentiality, integrity and availability of information and meets a defined requirement (glossary of NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final); ISO/IEC 27001 says "a measure that maintains or modifies risk". A control is always attached to a risk it reduces and a requirement that asks for it.

Category Preventive (stops before) Detective (discovers during/after) Corrective (limits damage, restores)
Technical (hardware or software) Inbound default deny on LAB-WIN11 (MC-09) Security log and auditd (MC-12) Restore of a database from a snapshot (MC-15)
Administrative (people following documented rules) Acceptable Use Policy §3 — never share an account (MC-16) Access review that finds a dormant account (MC-20) Disabling the account of a person who left (MC-17)
Physical (premises, equipment — mention only) Locked equipment room Store camera, recordings kept 30 days Replacing a tampered payment terminal

CompTIA Security+ splits administrative controls into managerial and operational, and adds deterrent, compensating and directive types. ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A themes map as follows: organizational and people → administrative; technological → technical; physical → physical. An IPS (intrusion prevention system) that blocks is preventive; an IDS (intrusion detection system) that only alerts is detective.

Three verification levels, always in the same order, and cumulative:

Level Meaning Typical evidence
Exists The control is present Feature visible, policy file, service in the list
Designed (design effectiveness) Configured or written so that it would meet the requirement Settings compared with the baseline; policy approved, dated, covering the requirement
Operating (operating effectiveness) Works as designed, every time, over a period Records through time: logs going back far enough, quarterly review records, a recent restore test, daily signature updates

A SOC 2 (System and Organization Controls 2) Type 1 report gives an opinion on the design of controls at one date; a Type 2 report also covers operating effectiveness over a period, usually 3 to 12 months (AICPA — American Institute of Certified Public Accountants: https://www.aicpa-cima.com/). NIST SP 800-53A collects evidence with examine, interview and test.

Which level does the requirement ask for? A requirement about a setting ("firewall on, inbound default deny", "volume fully encrypted") asks for designed. A requirement with a frequency, period or duration ("signatures updated daily", "logs retained 12 months", "restore tested every 6 months", "access reviewed every quarter") asks for operating. A control that only exists never meets a requirement on its own. Result = Conform when the level reached ≥ the level required, otherwise Non-conform, with the missing evidence in the Note.

Missing or empty evidence. Absence of records is not proof of operation: an incident register opened a year ago with 0 entries shows that the register exists, not that incidents are recorded — confirm with an interview and a test (ask how the last suspicious e-mail was handled, then look for it). When evidence cannot be collected, write Not tested — reason.

9.4 Validating network segmentation and zoning

Network segmentation divides a network into zones (systems with the same security needs) and controls the traffic between them. VLANs keep zones apart at layer 2 on shared switches; between VLANs, traffic passes through a firewall applying rules at layers 3 and 4. The zone policy is the approved list of flows (source zone, destination, service, reason); everything else is refused: default deny. Segmentation limits lateral movement, protects servers from guest devices and reduces PCI DSS scope.

Érable Cycles zone policy (Exhibit D) Allowed flow
Admin zone (admin VPN, 10.99.0.0/24) → servers Only TCP 3389 (RDP); → firewalls: only HTTPS (TCP 443)
OFFICE (VLAN 10) → SERVERS (VLAN 20) Only the Active Directory services of SRV-DC01 and SMB (Server Message Block, TCP 445) on SRV-FS01
POS (VLAN 30) → payment provider Only TCP 443; no other zone reaches the POS VLAN
GUEST Wi-Fi Internet only
Internet → internal networks Nothing (the web store runs in AWS, in the role of a DMZ — demilitarized zone)

Verification 1 — the firewall rule review (Exhibit E). A firewall applies the first rule that matches, from the top, so order matters. For each Allow rule check: approved flow; ticket and owner; no wider than needed (no "Any"); end date for temporary rules; default deny at the end, with logging. PCI DSS 1.2.7 asks for a review of network security control configurations at least every six months.

Verification 2 — the reachability test with Nmap (https://nmap.org/man/man-port-scanning-basics.html). Six port states exist; three matter here: open (a program accepts connections — SYN answered with SYN/ACK), closed (the host answers but nothing listens — RST), filtered (no useful reply, usually a firewall drops the probe). The others are unfiltered, open|filtered and closed|filtered. Command of step C3: nmap -Pn -p 22,80,135,139,443,445,3389 -oN <file> <LAB-WIN11-IP> — -Pn skips host discovery (Windows often blocks ping), -p lists the ports, -oN saves normal output, -oG saves grepable output. From the admin zone only TCP 3389 may reach a server; any other open port is a non-conformity.

Verification 3 — the configuration that creates the zones: interfaces and VLAN tags of firewall and switches. PCI DSS 11.4.5 asks for a penetration test of segmentation controls at least every 12 months when segmentation reduces scope — a different activity with its own scope; Lab 3 runs reachability checks only.

Authorization, targets and timing: scan only your own LAB-WIN11 from your own LAB-UBUNTU, inside the authorization period of your Lab 2 letter (November 11, 18:30 → November 19, 23:59 EST — Eastern Standard Time) and after 18:00. Both VMs must be on a host-only or NAT (network address translation) Network adapter. Nobody changes the firewall during the audit; recommended changes go to the corrective-action plan, outside the November 20–30 freeze.

9.5 Reviewing access control measures

Access control rests on identification (claim an identity), authentication (prove it), authorization (rights) and accountability (logs). Two principles guide every review: least privilege (only the rights the job needs) and separation of duties (no single person completes a sensitive task alone). 9.6 Cross-platform audit techniques

A heterogeneous environment (Windows, Linux, network devices, cloud) uses different commands, formats and words for the same idea (Windows says Block, ufw says deny; scripts say PASS/FAIL, consoles say compliant/non-compliant, a rule review says conform/violation). Four techniques make one answer possible to "How compliant are we?":

  1. One checklist, one command per platform — the requirement is written once, platform-independent, with one test procedure per platform (a crosswalk of commands); the pass rule stays the same.
  2. One normalized schema — eight columns (platform, host, item, check, native result, normalized verdict, evidence ID, framework references) and a mapping table: PASS, ok, compliant, conform → Conform; FAIL, fail, nonCompliant, violation → Non-conform. One form for host names (LAB-WIN11), one date format (ISO 8601, 2026-11-17), one time zone.
  3. Consistent evidence — naming rule L3-Exx_MC-yy_<what>_<host>, each file once in the evidence index with its SHA-256; NTP (Network Time Protocol) on every system so timestamps compare; language-independent identifiers such as SIDs.
  4. Rates, families and frameworks — conformity rate = Conform ÷ (Conform + Non-conform), by platform, by control family (firewall and segmentation, logging, endpoint, access) and overall; N/A and Not tested are kept out of the rate and reported next to it. Tonight's items map to CIS Controls 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13; ISO/IEC 27001 A.5.15, 5.18, 8.2, 8.5, 8.13, 8.15, 8.20, 8.22; PCI DSS requirements 1, 7, 8, 10.

Tools that do it at scale: SCAP and OpenSCAP, Wazuh (Windows, Linux, macOS), Intune compliance policies and AWS Config.

Exam traps — Chapter 9 - Exists → designed → operating: a setting requirement needs "designed"; anything with a frequency needs "operating". - An empty register does not prove that nothing happened. - A missing IDS is "not implemented", never N/A. - closed = RST (host answers, nothing listens); filtered = no useful reply. - 45 days (CIS 5.3) vs 90 days (PCI DSS 8.2.6); access review every 6 months (PCI DSS 7.2.4), quarterly at Érable Cycles. - N/A and Not tested are excluded from the conformity rate but reported next to it.

Case study 1

The stolen laptop in Laval

Covers: Chapter 2 › Lessons 2, 3 and 4 (PIPEDA breach rules, Law 25 confidentiality incidents, GDPR Art. 33–34) · Chapter 4 › Lesson 5 (decision table) · Chapter 9 › Lesson 2 (Encryption, MC-11)

Situation. On a Saturday, the laptop LT-023 of a sales associate is stolen from a car in Laval. It holds an export of 600 customer records (names, home addresses, e-mail addresses, order history) prepared for a promotion. The customers live in Québec and Ontario, and 40 of them live in France. The Intune encryption report shows LT-023 as "FullyEncrypted — protection suspended" since a firmware update three weeks ago.

Questions

  1. Which texts apply to this incident, and which regulator enforces each one?
  2. What must Érable Cycles assess and decide, and which records must it keep?
  3. What changes because 40 customers live in France?
  4. How is the laptop classified, and what does the encryption state mean for MC-11?

Most possible answers

  1. Law 25 applies because the enterprise holds personal information in Québec (regulator: the CAI, https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/). PIPEDA applies to the personal information that crosses provincial borders in a commercial activity, here the Ontario customers (regulator: the OPC, https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/). The GDPR applies to the data of the French customers because the company offers goods to people in the EU (Art. 3(2)); the competent authority would be an EU supervisory authority such as the CNIL (https://www.cnil.fr/en).
  2. Under Law 25 the company assesses the risk of serious injury with three factors (sensitivity, anticipated consequences, likelihood of injurious use); because protection was suspended, the data is readable, so the likelihood is high. If the risk is serious, it notifies the CAI and the persons concerned promptly, and in all cases enters the incident in the register (kept at least 5 years). Under PIPEDA it assesses the RROSH (sensitivity and probability of misuse), reports to the OPC and notifies the individuals as soon as feasible, notifies organizations that can reduce harm (the police), and keeps the breach record for 24 months.
  3. The GDPR adds a 72-hour clock: notify the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours (Art. 33), inform the French customers without undue delay if the risk to them is high (Art. 34), and document the breach (Art. 33(5)). Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj.
  4. The laptop stores personal information, so the decision table gives Confidential with the PI flag (Q3). MC-11 requires FullyEncrypted and ProtectionStatus On; "protection suspended" means the key is in clear on the disk, so MC-11 is Non-conform. If the suspension came from a fleet-wide update, it may be a major non-conformity; the corrective action is an Intune compliance rule that flags suspended protection, followed by a re-test of a sample of laptops. The auditor can also add an observation about exporting 600 customer records to a laptop at all (data minimisation).
Case study 2

Can the November newsletter go out?

Covers: Chapter 2 › Lesson 6 (CASL: CEM requirements, express and implied consent, unsubscribe) · Chapter 3 › Lesson 4 (retention of proof of consent)

Situation. The marketing team wants to send the November newsletter on November 16 to a list built as follows: 12,000 people who ticked an empty consent box on the web store; 3,000 customers who bought a bike 18 months ago and never subscribed; 1,500 people who asked a price question 9 months ago; 1,000 addresses bought from a partner; 500 people who unsubscribed 7 business days ago and are still on the list. The draft message has no mailing address of the company and its unsubscribe link points to a page that will be deleted on December 1.

Questions

  1. Which groups can receive the newsletter?
  2. What is wrong with the draft message itself?
  3. Which records must the company keep, and for how long?
  4. Who enforces these rules, and what is the maximum penalty?

Most possible answers

  1. The 12,000 gave express consent (an active tick, never a pre-checked box) — yes. The 3,000 have implied consent through an existing business relationship (purchase within 2 years) — yes. The 1,500 asked a question 9 months ago, past the 6-month limit for an inquiry — no, they need express consent. The 1,000 from a bought list have no relationship with Érable Cycles — no. The 500 unsubscribed: the request must take effect within 10 business days, and sending to them now would ignore an expressed choice — remove them before sending.
  2. Every CEM (commercial electronic message) needs three things: consent, identification of the sender with contact information (the missing mailing address is a defect), and an unsubscribe mechanism that works for at least 60 days after the message — a page deleted on December 1 would not last 60 days after November 16.
  3. The sender must be able to prove consent (s. 13), so consent records (date, method, wording shown) are kept for the life of the consent plus the 3-year limitation period, together with the unsubscribe requests and the date they took effect.
  4. The CRTC enforces CASL (https://www.crtc.gc.ca/eng/internet/CASL/spam.htm), with the Competition Bureau for misleading messages and the OPC for address harvesting. The maximum AMP is $10 million per violation for a business ($1 million for an individual).
Case study 3

Choosing the frameworks

Covers: Chapter 3 › Lessons 1, 2 and 3 (CSF 2.0, ISO/IEC 27001, CIS Controls v8.1, choosing for an SME) · Chapter 2 › Lesson 5 (PCI DSS scope)

Situation. The CEO asks you to recommend two frameworks. A consultant proposes ISO/IEC 27001 certification "within six months". The MSP says: "Just apply the CIS Benchmarks — that is a framework." Érable Cycles has 45 employees, one IT administrator, an MSP, no customer asking for a certificate, a hosted payment page and P2PE terminals.

Questions

  1. Which two frameworks do you recommend? Write the three-sentence justification.
  2. Why is PCI DSS not part of the choice?
  3. What is wrong with each of the two proposals?
  4. When would ISO/IEC 27001 become the right choice, and which document would become central?

Most possible answers

  1. CIS Controls v8.1 IG1 + a NIST CSF 2.0 Organizational Profile. Example: "Érable Cycles has 45 employees, one IT administrator and no customer requiring certification, so a free, prioritized list of safeguards is the practical reference: CIS Controls v8.1 IG1 (https://www.cisecurity.org/controls/v8-1). A NIST CSF 2.0 Profile (https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework) structures the report to the CEO and compares this year with last year. PCI DSS v4.0.1 applies in any case through the merchant agreement, with a scope reduced by the hosted payment page and the P2PE terminals."
  2. PCI DSS is not a choice: whenever cards are accepted, it applies by contract through the acquirer. The only decision is the scope (SAQ A-type for the web store, SAQ P2PE for the stores).
  3. The consultant: a first certificate commonly takes 12 to 18 months, costs consultant and certification-body fees and yearly surveillance, and no customer, partner or market requires it today. The MSP: CIS Benchmarks are per-product configuration values, not a framework for a whole program; they are used inside CIS Safeguard 4.1 and Lab 3 tests, not instead of the Controls.
  4. When a customer, a partner, a tender or a target market requires the certificate (for example if the web store starts selling to corporate fleets). Then Annex A becomes the reference and the Statement of Applicability (93 controls, applicable or not, justification, status) becomes the key document; certification is performed by a body accredited by the SCC (https://www.scc.ca/).
Case study 4

The inventory that does not match the network

Covers: Chapter 4 › Lessons 2, 3 and 6 (attributes, owner and custodian, source of truth, review cadence, change control, reconciliation)

Situation. You test the asset inventory on November 17. Its first page shows version 1.3, reviewed on August 2, 2026 (policy: every 90 days). nmap -sn on the lab subnet finds 14 hosts; the inventory lists 12 for that subnet, and one unknown host answers at 192.168.56.23. LT-017 now has a remote-access program that was not there at the last review, with no change ticket. The DB-RDS01 row names the IT Administrator as owner, and the payment provider row has an empty owner cell. A laptop decommissioned in September has disappeared from the file.

Questions

  1. Which tests reveal these problems?
  2. List the findings, with the requirement behind each one.
  3. Who should be the owner of DB-RDS01 and of the payment provider relationship?
  4. What should have happened to the decommissioned laptop's record?

Most possible answers

  1. The document test (version, date, reviewer, review within the cadence), the reconciliation from the inventory to reality (sample records, find the assets) and the reconciliation from reality to the inventory (hosts seen by nmap -sn found in the inventory).
  2. (a) Review overdue: August 2 + 90 days = October 31, so the November review is late — the inventory is not a current controlled document. (b) Host 192.168.56.23 has no record: an unauthorized asset (CIS Safeguard 1.2 — act on unauthorized assets weekly). (c) New software on LT-017 without a ticket: drift (CIS Safeguard 2.3 — unauthorized software). (d) Wrong or empty owners: ISO/IEC 27001 control 5.9 and CIS Safeguard 1.1 ask for owners; an empty owner cell is a finding in itself. (e) Deleted record: breaks traceability of the asset's data.
  3. DB-RDS01: the E-commerce Manager, the business role that decides who accesses customer data and accepts the risk (the IT Administrator or the MSP is the custodian; Amazon runs the hardware). Payment provider: the manager who signed the contract (Finance and Administration); the provider itself is the custodian.
  4. It should be kept and marked retired with the date and the change ticket, because the auditor asks what happened to the data it held (ISO/IEC 27001 controls 5.11 return of assets and 7.14 secure disposal).

Case study 6

Rating the findings

Covers: Chapter 5 › Lesson 4 (3×3 matrix, compensating controls, quantitative analysis) · Chapter 8 › Lessons 3 and 4 (severity, compensating control)

Situation. You must rate five non-conformities with the course matrix.

  • F1 — SSH root login allowed on the WEB-APP01 stand-in; SSH reachable from the Internet.
  • F2 — the same weakness on a second server, but SSH is reachable only from the admin VPN.
  • F3 — account lockout threshold of 10 on the laptops (rule: 5 or fewer).
  • F4 — the Security log of the domain controller stand-in covers 12 days; the requirement is 12 months (PCI DSS 10.5.1).
  • F5 — screen lock after 20 minutes instead of 15 on one laptop that never leaves the locked head office.

The CFO also asks whether a $4,000-per-year monitoring service is worth it against web store outages: one outage destroys 20 % of a $50,000 sales week, and one outage is expected every two years.

Questions

  1. Rate F1 to F5 (likelihood × impact → level) and order them.
  2. Why does F2 keep a Non-conform verdict?
  3. Compute SLE and ALE for the outage scenario. Is the $4,000 service justified on that basis alone?

Most possible answers

  1. F1: likelihood 3 (reachable from the Internet, no compensating control) × impact 3 (High-impact process, customer data) = 9 Critical. F4: likelihood 2 × impact 3 (a contractual duty is breached, so impact 3) = 6 High. F2: likelihood 2 (needs access to the admin VPN — partial compensating control) × impact 3 = 6 High. F3: 2 × 2 = 4 Medium. F5: likelihood 1 (needs physical access inside a locked office) × impact 2 = 2 Low. Order: F1, then F4 before F2 (same score; the legal or contractual deadline goes first), then F3, then F5.
  2. A compensating control lowers the likelihood but does not meet the requirement: "PermitRootLogin no" is still not true. The verdict stays Non-conform and the compensating control is written in the note.
  3. SLE = $50,000 × 0.20 = $10,000; ARO = 0.5; ALE = $10,000 × 0.5 = $5,000 per year. A control that removes most of this risk for $4,000 per year is economically justified on this basis, provided the input values are reliable (small companies rarely have such data, which is why the course uses the 3×3 matrix).
Case study 7

The Windows baseline on LAB-WIN11

Covers: Chapter 7 › Lessons 1, 2 and 3 (drift, baselines and tailoring, CIS-CAT Lite score, Policy Analyzer, MC-01)

Situation. CIS-CAT Lite (Windows 11 Enterprise v5.1.0, Level 1) reports 410 passed, 90 failed and 25 Manual recommendations. Among the failures: 9.3.1 (Public firewall profile state) and two recommendations in section 18. Policy Analyzer, comparing the Microsoft Windows 11 v26H2 baseline with the effective local policy, shows EnableFirewall = 0 for the Public profile and a lockout threshold of 10, equal to the Microsoft baseline. During Lab 0, the Public profile was on; no change ticket exists.

Questions

  1. Compute the CIS-CAT score.
  2. What is the MC-01 verdict, and why?
  3. How reliable is the firewall result?
  4. Which lockout threshold is expected, and is a threshold of 10 a finding?
  5. What do you call the firewall change since Lab 0, and what is the organization-level corrective action?

Most possible answers

  1. Score = passed ÷ (passed + failed) × 100 = 410 ÷ 500 × 100 = 82 %; the 25 Manual recommendations are listed but not scored.
  2. FAIL. The score is above 80 %, but the rule also requires no failed Level 1 recommendation in sections 1, 9 or 17; 9.3.1 is in section 9 (Firewall).
  3. Very reliable: two independent tools (CIS-CAT Lite and Policy Analyzer) report the same fact — corroborated evidence.
  4. The course keeps the stricter value: 5 or fewer (CIS Level 1) instead of Microsoft's 10, unless the risk owner documents a tailoring exception. A threshold of 10 is therefore Non-conform, even though it matches the Microsoft baseline.
  5. Configuration drift (a regression, PASS → FAIL) with no change ticket — a finding in itself. The organization should restore the setting in the baseline that configures every machine (a group policy on SRV-DC01 or an Intune profile), then re-test a sample; fixing one VM does not fix 45 laptops.
Case study 8

Is the web server really "locked down"?

Covers: Chapter 7 › Lessons 4 and 6 (Lynis, MC-02, patch verification) · Chapter 8 › Lessons 2 and 3 (effective value, ports, false negatives, compensating controls)

Situation. The MSP states that WEB-APP01 is "fully locked down". On the LAB-UBUNTU stand-in you collect: Lynis hardening index 64 with one warning (PKGS-7392, vulnerable packages); /etc/ssh/sshd_config contains "PermitRootLogin no", but sudo sshd -T prints "permitrootlogin without-password"; ss -tulnH shows 0.0.0.0:22, 127.0.0.53:53 and 0.0.0.0:631; apt list --upgradable shows 0 security updates, but the package lists were refreshed 11 days ago; systemctl is-enabled unattended-upgrades returns "disabled". The production SSH port is reachable only from the admin VPN.

Questions

  1. Give the verdict for MC-02, root login, ports (MC-06) and automatic updates (MC-03).
  2. Which result might be a false negative, and how do you check it?
  3. What does the admin-VPN restriction change?

Most possible answers

  1. MC-02 FAIL (index 64 < 70 and one warning). Root login FAIL: the effective value wins over the main file; without-password is the older name of prohibit-password (key login allowed), which is not "no" — a drop-in file read first sets it. MC-06 FAIL: 0.0.0.0:631 listens on every interface, and only 22/tcp is allowed; 127.0.0.53:53 is loopback and is ignored. MC-03 automatic updates FAIL: unattended-upgrades is disabled, while the Patch Management Procedure requires it for WEB-APP01.
  2. "0 security updates waiting" from 11-day-old package lists may be a false negative. Run sudo apt update, re-perform the check and record the age of the lists in the note; the Lynis warning about vulnerable packages already suggests that updates are missing.
  3. It is a compensating control: it lowers the likelihood when rating the root-login finding, but the verdict stays Non-conform. The statement "fully locked down" was only partly true — exactly why the auditor collects evidence instead of accepting statements.
Case study 9

Black Friday versus automatic updates

Covers: Chapter 8 › Lessons 4, 5 and 6 (findings table, exceptions, deviation register, Check → Act)

Situation. Two exception requests arrive. EX-03 — the E-commerce Manager asks to keep automatic updates off on WEB-APP01 from November 20 to December 1, because a failed update could break the checkout during the change freeze. Compensating controls: SSH reachable only from the admin VPN, a snapshot every night, critical patches installed manually after a test. Risk before: High (6); after: Medium (4). Owner: E-commerce Manager. Approver: CEO. End date: December 1. Review date: December 2. EX-04 — the IT Administrator asks to keep the shared account support.client "because users complain about having to sign in". No end date, no compensating control, no risk rating; he approved it himself.

Questions

  1. Decide APPROVED or REJECTED for each request, with reasons.
  2. What is written in the findings table for the WEB-APP01 updates finding?
  3. What happens on December 1 and 2?
  4. How is the fix verified after updates are switched back on?

Most possible answers

  1. EX-03 APPROVED: all eight elements are present (finding and requirement, specific business reason, compensating control, risk before and after with a non-Critical residual, owner, independent approver with authority, end date within 90 days, review date). EX-04 REJECTED: convenience is not a business reason; there is no compensating control, no risk rating, no end date and no review date, and the requester approved his own request (no independence). It stays in the deviation register with the list of missing elements, and the shared account remains a finding (PCI DSS 8.2.2; Acceptable Use Policy §3).
  2. The finding stays Non-conform, with the note "exception approved until 2026-12-01 (EX-03)". An exception never turns a finding into Conform.
  3. At the end date there are two outcomes only: the requirement is met and verified (the deviation is closed), or a new request goes through the eight elements. An expired exception is reported as an open non-conformity.
  4. Under change control (evidence saved, snapshot, one ticket naming the check), switch unattended-upgrades back on, re-run the same script and compare: L03 must move FAIL → PASS and nothing else may change (no regression, no unplanned change). The comparison is kept as evidence.
Case study 10

Part C night: controls, segmentation and access

Covers: Chapter 9 › Lessons 1 to 6 (verification levels, technical controls, segmentation, access review, conformity rate)

Situation. Evidence collected on November 17: - Get-NetFirewallProfile -PolicyStore ActiveStore: Domain On/Block, Private On/Block, Public On/Allow. - Nmap from LAB-UBUNTU (playing the admin zone) to LAB-WIN11: 3389 open, 445 open, 135 filtered, 22 closed. The last signed access review is dated May 2026 (policy: every quarter).

Questions

  1. Give the level reached and the verdict for MC-09, MC-10, MC-12, MC-19 and MC-20.
  2. Which requirements in this list ask for the operating level?
  3. Compute the conformity rate of these five items, overall and by control family.

Most possible answers

  1. MC-09 Non-conform (designed level not reached: the Public profile's default inbound action is Allow, not Block). MC-10 Conform (operating: real-time protection on and signatures 0 days old). MC-12 Non-conform (designed — events are written — but not operating: 12 days instead of 12 months for PCI DSS 10.5.1, and below CIS 8.10's 90 days). MCQ 1Covers: Chapter 1 — Introduction to Conformity Assessment › Lesson 1 — Definition and objectives of conformity assessment (key terms)
According to ISO/IEC 17000:2020 (International Organization for Standardization / International Electrotechnical Commission), what is conformity assessment?
Hint: The definition is built on three words: specified, requirements, fulfilled.

Answer: c) The demonstration that specified requirements are fulfilled

Explanation: ISO/IEC 17000:2020 (https://www.iso.org/standard/73029.html) defines conformity assessment as the demonstration that specified requirements are fulfilled: an evidence-based comparison between what is required and what exists. A penetration test tries to break in; it does not compare evidence with requirements. Certification is only one possible outcome, issued by a third party (not necessarily a public authority). A list of chosen controls describes something like a Statement of Applicability, not an assessment.

MCQ 2Covers: Chapter 1 — Introduction to Conformity Assessment › Lesson 1 — Definition and objectives (non-conformity, major / minor)
The auditor finds that Érable Cycles has no disk-encryption policy at all and that none of the 45 laptops is encrypted. How is this recorded?
Hint: Is the control missing completely, or did a working control fail once?

Answer: b) Major non-conformity

Explanation: A non-conformity is major when the control is absent or has broken down; here there is neither a policy nor a single encrypted laptop. A minor non-conformity would be an isolated lapse in a control that otherwise works (one laptop out of ten without encryption). An observation is used only when no requirement is broken, and nothing here conforms.

Which term is used for the fulfilment of mandatory external obligations such as laws, regulations and contracts?

Answer: d) Compliance

Explanation: In ISO usage, conformity is the fulfilment of any requirement (internal policy, standard, contract, law), while compliance is used for mandatory external obligations. Answer: c) A law and the regulations adopted under it

A fully patched system that has drifted from its baseline is still non-conform. NIST SP 800-40 Rev. 4: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/40/r4/final.

Why this answer works: It lists the four facts with evidence sources and explains the link between patching and drift.

Essay 16Covers: Chapter 8 — Software configuration audits, part 2 › Lesson 3 — Reading and interpreting the results
Explain false positives and false negatives, corroboration and compensating controls, using one example of each.
Hint: Which error hides a problem? What changes the likelihood but not the verdict?

Model answer: A false positive is a FAIL reported while the system really conforms: W05 reports "screen lock not configured" because a registry value is absent, but Intune enforces the lock by device policy. A false negative is a PASS reported while the system does not conform: L02 reports "0 updates waiting" from 9-day-old package lists. The false negative is the more dangerous one, because the problem stays hidden. Both come from the measurement (wrong source, old data, missing rights). Corroboration confirms a doubtful result with a second, independent source: the Intune policy report or a timed idle test for W05; sudo apt update and a re-run for L02. A compensating control is a different measure that reduces the same risk when the required control is missing: root may sign in over SSH with a key, but SSH is reachable only from the admin VPN. The verdict stays Non-conform, the compensating control goes in the note, and it may lower the likelihood when the finding is rated.

Why this answer works: Each concept has a correct definition and a matching example from the course data, and the answer explains which error is more dangerous.

Essay 17Covers: Chapter 8 — Software configuration audits, part 2 › Lesson 5 — Deviations and exceptions; Lesson 6 — Closing the loop
Distinguish non-conformity, deviation, exception and risk acceptance, and explain how a fix is verified once the exception ends.
Hint: A fact, a record, a permission, a decision — then re-run and compare.

Model answer: A non-conformity is a fact found by the audit: a requirement is not met (automatic updates off on WEB-APP01). A deviation is that difference recorded in the deviation register with an owner, tracked until closed. An exception is a formal, approved, time-limited permission not to meet the requirement, with compensating controls — valid only with all eight elements: finding and requirement, business reason, compensating control, risk before and after, owner, an approver with authority who is not the requester, an end date (90 days at most) and a review date. Risk acceptance is the informed decision, by a person with authority such as the CEO, to keep the known risk. The finding stays Non-conform with the exception ID. When the exception ends, the change is made under change control (evidence saved, snapshot, one ticket naming the check), the same script is re-run and compared: the planned check must move FAIL → PASS and nothing else may change. Only then is the deviation closed.

Why this answer works: It separates the four terms clearly, lists the eight elements and describes verification as a before-and-after comparison.

Essay 18Covers: Chapter 9 — Verification of security controls › Lesson 1 — Controls and how to verify them
Explain the three verification levels of a control and how the wording of a requirement decides the level required. Give two examples.
Hint: Exists → designed → operating; a setting versus a frequency.

Model answer: A control is verified at three cumulative levels. Exists: the control is present (the antivirus is installed, the policy is written). Designed: it is configured or written so that it would meet the requirement (real-time protection on; policy approved and dated). Operating: it works as designed over a period, proven by dated records (signatures updated every day, quarterly review records). The requirement's wording decides the level required. A requirement about a setting — "firewall on, inbound default deny" — needs the designed level, and a correct configuration on the test date is enough. A requirement with a frequency or a period — "logs retained 12 months" — needs the operating level: a 20 MB Security log that covers 12 days is designed but not operating, so MC-12 is Non-conform. A control that only exists never meets a requirement. The result is Conform when the level reached is at least the level required. SOC 2 reports follow the same logic: Type 1 for design, Type 2 for operation over a period.

Why this answer works: It defines the three levels, gives the rule for the level required and supports it with two correct examples.

Essay 19Covers: Chapter 9 — Verification of security controls › Lesson 4 — Validating network segmentation and zoning
Describe how the auditor validates network segmentation at Érable Cycles and how the Nmap results are interpreted.
Hint: Zone policy, rule review, reachability test, configuration; open, closed, filtered.

Model answer: Segmentation divides the network into zones (admin VPN, office, servers, POS, guest Wi-Fi) and controls the traffic between them with a zone policy and default deny. The auditor uses three verifications. (1) Firewall rule review: the firewall applies the first matching rule, so for each Allow rule the auditor checks the approved flow, the ticket and owner, that it is no wider than needed, an end date for temporary rules, and a default deny at the end; PCI DSS 1.2.7 asks for this review every six months. (2) Reachability test with Nmap (https://nmap.org/): from LAB-UBUNTU to LAB-WIN11, nmap -Pn -p 22,80,135,139,443,445,3389 -oN file. Open means a program accepts connections, closed means the host answered with a reset, filtered means no useful reply. From the admin zone, only TCP 3389 may be open; any other open port is a non-conformity. (3) Configuration review of the interfaces and VLAN tags. The scan runs only on the student's own VMs, inside the authorization period, after 18:00 and outside the change freeze; it is a reachability check, not a penetration test.

Why this answer works: It covers the three verifications, interprets each port state against the policy and respects the legal limits of testing.

Essay 20Covers: Chapter 9 — Verification of security controls › Lesson 5 — Reviewing access control measures; Lesson 6 — Cross-platform audit techniques
Describe how an access review is verified and how results from several platforms are combined into one conformity rate.
Hint: Account types, thresholds, JML, signed record; then normalization and the rate formula.

Model answer: The auditor compares three lists: the people from HR (with start and end dates), the accounts (enabled, last sign-in) and the group memberships. Findings include enabled accounts of people who left (PCI DSS 8.2.5 asks for immediate revocation), shared accounts without an approved exception (8.2.2), administrators using their privileged account for e-mail (CIS 5.4), and dormant accounts — days of inactivity compared with 45 days (CIS 5.3) and 90 days (PCI DSS 8.2.6), the stricter deciding the first action. The access review itself must be recorded and signed: every six months under PCI DSS 7.2.4, every quarter in the company policy; no record means no review. To report one number, results from Windows, Linux, the firewall and the cloud are normalized: one schema, and a mapping table that turns PASS, ok and compliant into Conform, and FAIL, nonCompliant and violation into Non-conform. The conformity rate = Conform ÷ (Conform + Non-conform), by platform, by control family and overall; N/A and Not tested rows are excluded from the rate but reported next to it.

Why this answer works: It joins the access-control rules with the reporting technique and gives the exact formula and its exclusions.
